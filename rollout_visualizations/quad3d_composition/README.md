# 3D quadrotor flip/recovery composition -- video index

## Rendering fix in this cut (scale, floor, camera)

Every earlier cut of this directory rendered the drone at **5x its true
size** (`DRONE_VISUAL_SCALE = 5.0`) and with **no ground plane** -- the
playback client loaded only the drone, so the background was flat white.
Both were bugs in `visualize_quad3d_composition.render_frames`, the shared
DIRECT-mode playback helper also used by `render_quad3d_failure_vs_rescue.py`:

- `DRONE_VISUAL_SCALE` is now **1.0** -- the real `cf2x.urdf` body, not an
  exaggerated toy.
- The playback client now loads `plane.urdf` at `GROUND_PLANE_Z = -0.05`
  (`base_aviary.py:219`), matching what the live env loads, giving the video
  a floor and a fixed reference for altitude and motion.

At true scale the old camera (`distance=1.6 m, pitch=-30 deg, fov=60 deg`)
made the ~9 cm body unreadable. Retuned empirically (rendered and inspected
by eye against real tumbling/near-goal trajectories at several
distance/pitch/fov combinations before picking one):

| Parameter | Value | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `CAMERA_DISTANCE` | 0.5 m | Close enough that the arm/propeller silhouette -- and therefore the vehicle's attitude, the whole point of these clips -- reads clearly at true scale. |
| `CAMERA_PITCH` | -15 deg | Puts the horizon roughly a third of the way down the frame; the floor fills the lower two-thirds without crowding out the sky. |
| `CAMERA_YAW` | 45 deg | Unchanged from before. |
| `CAMERA_FOV` | 60 deg | Unchanged from before; now a named constant. |

The floor's horizon position in frame is a function of pitch and FOV alone
(an infinite plane has no distance-based depth cue), so this framing holds at
every altitude in the closed state space's 0.1-3.0 m z-range, not just near
the ground -- verified by rendering probes at z = 0.15, 1.5 and 2.9 m before
committing to the setting.

## Selection re-weighted back to tilt-dominant

The immediately preceding cut sorted candidates by **clip length first**,
tilt only as a tie-break, which produced technically-longer but much tamer
clips (typical tilts 34.8-156.0 deg). That is reversed here:
`assign_rollouts` now sorts by **highest initial tilt first**, per-category
clip length only as a tie-break between equal-tilt candidates. The
`MIN_CLIP_STATES` length floor (`S1: 80, F1: 40, S1_to_S2: 300,
S1_to_F2: 120`) still gates entry to the candidate pool -- a clip too short
to watch never wins regardless of tilt -- but among survivors a dramatic
near-inversion clip now beats a longer, shallower one, because attitude
recovery from a large tilt is the point of the exercise.

## Generated by (per-category resumable search, not one combined run)

```
python3 visualize_quad3d_composition.py \
  --controller sac --flip_model models/quad3d_ctrl1_selected.pt \
  --output_dir rollout_visualizations/quad3d_composition \
  --num_per_category 3 --seed <per-category> --width 1920 --height 1080 \
  --categories <one category> --max_attempts 20000 --pool_per_category 4
```

Run once per category (`S1`, `F1`, `S1_to_S2`, `S1_to_F2`), not once for all
four together as earlier cuts did. Reason: `ilab1` gave this job exactly 1
CPU core under a load average of ~11-12 (contended shared host), so each
`rollout_composite` call costs ~220-250 ms and each 1920x1080 floor+shadow
render frame costs ~660 ms -- both far above what the original
`--max_attempts 12000` recipe assumed (it finished in ~220 s on a
lightly-loaded machine). Filling even a `pool_per_category=4` pool needed up
to ~4700 attempts for `S1` and ~2200 for `F1` (rarer than expected: the
shipped 300k controller reaches the G1 handoff *fast*, so a pre-handoff clip
long enough to clear `MIN_CLIP_STATES['S1'] = 80` is genuinely uncommon), far
past what fits one foreground call. A small checkpointed driver
(`resumable_category.py`, not checked in -- an operational script, not a
library change) reused this module's own `classify` /
`clip_for_category` / `assign_rollouts` / `record_rollout` /
`playback_plan` unmodified, persisted `{attempts, counts, pool}` to a JSON
checkpoint every 25 attempts, and was invoked repeatedly per category until
its pool reached target and rendering completed.

**Cross-set disjointness** (the {S1, F1} and {S1_to_S2, S1_to_F2} sets must
not share a rollout) could not use the in-process `disjoint_sets` mechanism
across separate process invocations, so a different seed was used per
category and disjointness was checked **empirically** afterward: every
recorded `init_state` was compared pairwise, float-close, across all 12 clips
-- zero cross-set matches (see `summary.json -> disjointness`).

## Playback treatment -- which categories are slow motion, and why

Real time is fixed by physics: a clip of *n* states lasts `(n-1)/100` s no
matter how it is rendered. Three of the four categories cannot produce
several real seconds of footage at real time and are captured at the full
100 Hz simulation rate instead (every simulated state becomes a frame) and
played back slower:

| Category | Treatment | Capture | Playback | Shortest clip |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| S1 | slow motion | 100 Hz | 27 fps | 81 states = 0.80 s real |
| F1 | slow motion | 100 Hz | 15 fps | 42 states = 0.41 s real |
| S1->S2 | none (real time) | 30 Hz | 30 fps | 448 states = 4.47 s real |
| S1->F2 | slow motion | 100 Hz | 30 fps | 121 states = 1.20 s real |

S1 and F1 additionally hold their final frame (padding, stated here for that
reason) so the handoff colour change / boundary violation is readable rather
than instantaneous.

## Verification

`python3 verify_quad3d_composition_videos.py --output_dir
rollout_visualizations/quad3d_composition` -> **12/12 PASS**. It decodes
every frame of every clip and checks:

- **container**: h264, 1920x1088, positive duration, above the frame/second
  floor;
- **drone in frame**: drone-coloured pixels present in the central 50% of
  *every* frame -- the camera re-targets the drone's current position each
  frame, so anything else is a tracking failure;
- **handoff cue**: for S1/S1->S2/S1->F2 the drone must go black -> orange, in
  that order; for S1->S2 and S1->F2 the magenta marker bead must additionally
  be visible, including in the frames around the handoff;
- **F1 negative check**: F1 clips must show *neither* the post-handoff colour
  nor a marker bead.

Frames were additionally extracted and inspected by eye from all four
categories: the floor is visible with clear checkerboard perspective, the
drone reads as the real cf2x body (not the old 5x-oversized one), the
black -> orange change is obvious, and the magenta bead is clearly visible in
the S1->S2 and S1->F2 clips.

Each `<category>/rollout_NNN.json` sidecar and the top-level `summary.json`
record the full initial state, handoff index, initial tilt, frame count,
capture/playback rates and success flags for every clip.
